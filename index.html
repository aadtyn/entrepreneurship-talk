sensemaking

school fest. you run a food stall.
people stop, read the menu, leave.

your guess: too expensive.

what happens if you cut prices right now?

instead you listen. "we'll miss the next event." the queue is 15 minutes.

the problem was never the price. it was the wait.

test: one grab-and-go item, same price. if it sells, you were right.

stop at the yellow question. let them answer: you lose money AND still don't know why they left. the signal was the wait, not the price. sensemaking = finding the real signal before you act. then test it cheaply.

bridge into failure modes. keep the labels hidden until they answer. compliments mistaken for demand = losing touch with reality / vitamin not painkiller. friend as cofounder without testing how they work = wrong people. spending the last money out of frustration = tilt. inventory before checking demand = running out of fuel.

if you just don't die,
you're ahead of most people.

there are so many ways to die that survival IS the strategy.

but don't be scared of it. failing is the fastest way to learn.
fail small and fast. not big and slow.

failure mode 1 · losing touch with reality

month 11.
zero customers.
"it just needs one more feature"
technical people build build build and never talk to a customer. then they die. say: "i've done this." + your one-liner story. highest-trust moment in the talk.

the customer writes the syllabus. not you.

pressure on a founder is enormous. if your nervous system can't hold uncertainty you fumble at the exact moment it matters. nervous system mastery = anti-tilt training.
